Nesting

At quantity we lay parts out to fill the bed rather than printing them one at a time. The same eight hours produces twelve pieces instead of one.

Material buying

Filament and resin bought in for a specific run costs less per kilo than stock pulled off the shelf.

Setup amortised

File prep, slicing, test print and first-article inspection are a fixed cost. Across 500 pieces it disappears.

Machines · Materials · Tolerances

What the workshop can actually do

The honest version: what we print on, what we print with, how accurate it is, and where 3D printing is the wrong answer.

FDM — fused filament

The workhorse. Strong, economical, available in a wide range of engineering and decorative filaments. Best for functional parts, larger pieces, jigs, enclosures and volume gifting. Layer heights from 0.08 to 0.32 mm.

Resin — SLA / MSLA

For detail the eye can see. Miniatures, jewellery masters, dental and medical models, textured surfaces and anything where a layer line would ruin it. Layer heights from 0.02 to 0.05 mm.

Materials

Pick by what the part has to survive

Most people over-specify. A desk piece does not need nylon; a part that lives in a parked car in May absolutely does not want PLA. This is the short version of that conversation.

Accuracy

What tolerance you can hold us to

Printed parts are not machined parts. These are the numbers we will put in writing — if your drawing needs tighter than this on a critical feature, tell us and we will either machine that feature after printing or tell you it is the wrong process.

  • FDM dimensional: ±0.3 mm or ±0.3% of the dimension, whichever is greater
  • Resin dimensional: ±0.1 mm or ±0.15%, whichever is greater
  • Minimum wall: 1.2 mm on FDM, 0.6 mm on resin
  • Minimum readable text: 2.5 mm embossed on FDM, 1 mm on resin
  • Holes: print undersized by design; reamed or drilled to size when the fit is critical
  • Threads: heat-set brass inserts recommended over printed threads for anything that is undone more than once

Build volume

Up to 300 × 300 × 400 mm in a single FDM piece, and up to 220 × 120 × 250 mm in resin. Larger objects are split, printed in sections and joined — a joint line, planned properly, is invisible.

Inspection

Vernier and micrometer checks against your drawing, fit tests against a mating part where one exists, and a photograph set with every dispatch.

Post-processing

Support removal, sanding, priming, spray and hand painting, vapour smoothing on ABS, gluing and multi-part assembly, insert setting, polishing and clear coating.

Files we accept

STL, OBJ, 3MF, STEP, IGES, and native SolidWorks or Fusion files. Or a photo, a sketch, a drawing, a PDF, or the physical part itself for us to measure.

Honesty section

When 3D printing is the wrong answer

We would rather lose a job than take one that was always going to disappoint you.

Very high volumes

Past roughly ten to fifteen thousand identical simple parts, injection moulding usually wins on unit cost even after the tooling. We will say so — and we can print the pre-tooling pilot run that de-risks that mould.

Food, skin and load-critical parts

Layer lines trap bacteria, and a printed part in a safety-critical load path is not something to guess at. We will suggest a food-safe liner, a coating, or a different process.

Very large single pieces

Above the build volume, a part is printed in sections and bonded. That is often perfect — but if a seamless one-metre piece is the requirement, casting or fabrication is the honest answer.

That is the whole business, really: take the idea seriously, whatever size it arrives in.
