How an OEM engagement runs

Six stages, and you always know which one you are in

01

NDA and brief

Mutual NDA, then a written brief: what the part is, quantities, target landed cost, packaging and the date it must exist by.

02

Feasibility and costing

We confirm it is printable at your target cost, or tell you what would have to change. You get a costed quotation with volume breaks.

03

Golden sample

One approved physical piece, photographed and measured. It becomes the reference every batch is judged against.

04

Specification freeze

Material, colour, layer height, infill, finish, tolerance, packaging and inspection points, written down and signed.

05

Pilot batch

A small production run against the frozen spec, inspected and dispatched, to prove the process before the volume commits.

06

Rolling production

Repeat batches on your call-off schedule, each with an inspection record and dispatch photographs.

What we need from you

  • The 3D file (STL, STEP, OBJ or 3MF) — or a sample part we can measure and model
  • A drawing or note marking any dimension that is critical, and to what tolerance
  • Quantity, and whether it is one-off, recurring or a call-off schedule
  • Colour, finish and any brand-critical reference
  • Packaging requirement — bulk, individually bagged, boxed, or your own artwork
  • The date it must be delivered by, and whether that date is hard
Vendor registration. If your company needs us on your approved-vendor list first, ask and we will send the GST registration, PAN, bank details and a company profile in whatever format your procurement portal wants.

Bulk orders

What volume actually changes

Three things drive the per-unit price: how long a piece occupies a machine, how much material it eats, and how much handling it needs afterwards. Volume attacks all three.

Nesting

At quantity we lay parts out to fill the bed rather than printing them one at a time. The same eight hours produces twelve pieces instead of one.

Material buying

Filament and resin bought in for a specific run costs less per kilo than stock pulled off the shelf.

Setup amortised

File prep, slicing, test print and first-article inspection are a fixed cost. Across 500 pieces it disappears.
